# Results — decomposition as an operator (run 2026-10-09/10, protocol v2)

560 jobs, 0 failed, $5.07 nominal (wave-1 pilot under protocol v1 another
$0.66, 186 records, `runs-wave1-protocol1.jsonl`, not pooled). PREREG.md fixed before the
run; the v2 protocol revision is recorded there. Raw: `runs.jsonl`; tables:
`STATS.md` (generated by stats.py). Arm 1 and best-of-k: the corpus's 16
draws per item (corpus-traces, audited labels). Budget: 8,192 completion
tokens per decomposition attempt, same as one corpus draw.

## Verdict

**H0 holds where it can be tested; the apparent gains are budget and guessing.**

1. **True hard, no cap (DeepSeek, 20 items the model solves ≤ 4/16 with no
   truncation).** On the 14 numeric items (olympiad 10, GSM-Hard 4):
   decomposition pass@4 0.21 = single pass@4 0.21 (Δ 0.00 [−0.29, +0.29]);
   eight decomposition attempts add **no item** to the single model's
   pass@16 (union +0.00 [0, 0]). Olympiad 0/16 items: 0 of 7 solved in 8
   attempts each. On the 6 GPQA items decomposition "solves" 3 of 5 zero
   items, but at a per-attempt rate of **0.23 over 40 attempts, chance for
   four options is 0.25**: the single model is confidently wrong 16 times,
   decomposition breaks that path and guesses. The PREREG signal "≥ 3 of
   the 12 audited 0/16 items solved" is met nominally (3) and entirely by
   GPQA at chance rate; the numeric half is 0 of 7. Not a signal.
2. **Hard because of the cap (GLM, 38 items with ≥ 8/16 draws truncated).**
   Decomposition pass@4 0.71 vs single pass@4 0.37 (+0.34 [+0.18, +0.50]),
   above single pass@16 (0.58). Predicted direction, larger size. On GPQA
   the per-attempt rate on 0/16 items is 0.47, above chance; on numeric
   items union over single@16 is +0.09 [0, +0.23]. The model that cannot
   finish one long pass finishes four short ones. That is budget
   reallocation, not new support: the same model with its cap removed is
   the right comparator and is not in the corpus.
3. **Medium and easy.** GLM: +0.06 [−0.13, +0.22], no loss. DeepSeek:
   **−0.41 [−0.60, −0.21]**: it solves inside the plan call (34 of 98 arm-2
   runs lose both plan calls to length), overruns every subtask (62 % of its arm-2
   subtask calls truncated vs 22 % for GLM), and re-solves from scratch in
   the assembly, which then truncates without a FINAL line (34 of 98). A
   model that does not compress its reasoning to the step size pays for
   decomposition at equal budget. This is the Interaction Tax result, here
   for a single model split against itself.
4. **Team vs split.** Arm 3 (subtasks to Qwen 3.7 plus, Kimi K2.5, Mistral
   Large; 53 of 927 subtask calls re-routed after 429s) is never above arm
   2 outside GPQA: DeepSeek hard −0.15 [−0.30, 0.00], GLM hard-cap numeric
   −0.18 [−0.41, 0.00]; GLM GPQA +0.12 [−0.13, +0.38]. The operator is
   "split", not "team"; hand-off between models costs.
5. **Correlation.** φ(arm 2, single draw 0): GLM 0.58, DeepSeek 0.22 (vs
   φ between two single draws 0.53 / 0.68). Decomposition's errors are
   less tied to the single pass's than a resample's are, which is exactly
   what a change of budget allocation plus guessing produces.

## Predictions vs observed
- hard-true arm2 pass@1 ≤ 0.15: observed 0.20 (0.00 on numeric). Within
  noise; the excess is GPQA guessing.
- 0/16 items solved 0–1 of 12: observed 3, all GPQA at chance. The PREREG
  did not set a guessing floor for 8 attempts on 4-option items; it should
  have (expected under pure guessing: 1 − 0.75⁸ ≈ 0.9 of items "solved at
  least once"). The numeric prediction (0–1) held: 0 of 7.
- hard-cap: predicted 0.2–0.4 per attempt, observed 0.26 numeric / 0.66
  GPQA; predicted "signal of more" = decomposition pass@1 above single
  pass@4: 0.45 vs 0.37, yes on the pooled stratum, driven by GPQA.
- medium within ±0.10: GLM yes, DeepSeek no (−0.41).
- easy loses ≤ 2 of 6: GLM 0, DeepSeek 3.
- arm 3 ≤ arm 2: yes outside GPQA.
- φ 0.3–0.6: GLM 0.58 yes, DeepSeek 0.22 below.

## What this closes and what it does not
Closes OPEN-QUESTIONS #1 for the inference-time case on this corpus:
splitting a task, within one model or across three, at equal budget, does
not solve what the model cannot solve in sixteen whole attempts, on numeric
problems with no guessing floor. It helps a model that runs out of tokens,
by the amount a larger budget would, and it hurts a model that does not
shorten its steps. Not tested: decomposition with a verifier per step
(the partial-verifier question, #2); decomposition with tools; a single
model with its cap removed as the comparator for the hard-cap stratum;
more than four subtasks; a planner model different from the solver.

## Limits
- 20 true-hard items on one strong model; 14 numeric. Intervals are wide
  (±0.29 on the numeric Δ). The null is "no item gained", which is exact on
  this set, not an estimate.
- GLM's hard stratum is a cap artefact of the corpus's 8,192 limit; the
  stratum answers a budget question, not a capability question.
- The budget rule is a token cap, not matched spend; see Dollars. For
  DeepSeek decomposition cost 3.5–9× a whole draw and still gained nothing;
  for GLM it cost half, because reasoning was bounded per call, which is a
  confound for the cap-stratum gain (no reasoning-capped single pass was run).
- Two protocol revisions before the main run (reasoning bound, plan prompt)
  are recorded in PREREG; the first wave under protocol v1 is kept as a
  pilot. No change after the main run started.
- Arm 3 lost Mistral to rate limits on 53 of 927 subtask calls and
  substituted the next worker; recorded per call.
- Grading is the corpus grader (FINAL: line, numeric tolerance, letter);
  decomposition attempts without a FINAL line count as wrong (DeepSeek
  assembly truncations), which is the equal-budget rule applied strictly.

Reading. DeepSeek fills the per-call caps, so one decomposition attempt
costs 3.5× one whole draw on hard problems and 8–9× on medium/easy (a
whole draw there is 900–2,600 tokens). At equal money the single model's
pass@k edges decomposition on the pooled hard stratum (0.38 vs 0.35) and
beats it on the numeric part (0.39 vs 0.21) and everywhere else (1.00 vs
0.25–0.50). The token-cap comparison favoured decomposition and it still
gained nothing; the dollar comparison is worse for it. GLM is the mirror:
its single draw on cap-hard problems burns the whole 7,735-token cap on
reasoning, while a decomposition attempt, with reasoning bounded to half
of each call, spends 2,987 tokens and half the money, and is better (0.45
vs 0.10 at one draw's money; 0.71 vs 0.18 at two draws'). So for GLM
decomposition is cheaper *and* better on the cap stratum — but the
bounded-reasoning rule is a confound: a single pass with reasoning capped
at 4,096 might do the same for less, and was not run. Arm 3 is 2–17× a
draw because Qwen plus, Kimi and Mistral cost more per token.

Net: in money, decomposition loses on every stratum for the model that
does not overrun, and wins for the model that does only because the
protocol forced it to think less per call.

GLM alone: 0–7 truncated (21 items) +0.03…+0.14; 12–15 (23) +0.46; 16 (13)
+0.15. DeepSeek alone: 0 truncated (28) −0.09; 1–7 (10) −0.17.

The gain is a function of the single pass overrunning its budget, not of
the model's benchmark level: the stronger model (DeepSeek, no overruns)
gains nothing or loses; the weaker one gains exactly on the items where it
is cut off, and not on the items it finishes. Items cut on every draw are
the exception on the numeric side (0.00 on 5): where the budget is far
short, pieces do not finish either. Two models only; the within-model
gradient is the evidence that the mediator is overrun, not identity.
